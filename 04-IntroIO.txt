3. Reading/Writing I/O
There are functions to configure I/O; we will use these later for more advanced I/O.
Check the pins on your ESP32 to determine which pins can be used for input versus output and digital versus analog.
For digital I/O: digitalRead and digitalWrite.
For analog I/O: analogRead and analogWrite.
4. Abstraction via a device driver
Abstraction
Allows us to modularize our code.
Gives us the option to expose what we want users to see and hide what we don't want them to see.
Device Driver
Abstraction is used to expose public routines that we want users of the driver to call and use private routines to hide driver internals from the user (more on private routines later).
For example, rather than using digitalWrite with specific values to turn on or off an LED, an LED device driver could specify:
LED_Init
LED_Off
LED_On
LED_Toggle
It is very useful to have a well-designed device driver for complex I/O devices.

Use power rails (columns) for V~DD~ and ground.
Your ESP32, other ICs, and buttons must straddle the center valley.
Other components, e.g., resistors, LEDs, buttons, etc., need to use multiple rows.
